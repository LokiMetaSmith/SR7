# Hollow Resonance Worldbuilding Guide

## Section 1: Introduction & Themes

> **GM Note: Stopgap Rules**
> Until the complete "Fan made Shadowrun 7th Edition" homebrew ruleset is finalized, GMs are strongly advised to run the *Hollow Resonance* campaign using the standard Shadowrun 5th Edition rules. The custom 7E homebrew concepts should be used strictly for narrative flavor and worldbuilding rather than enforced mechanical systems.

Welcome to Zkazena, the blighted city. This document serves as a comprehensive guide for Game Masters and writers looking to craft narratives within the fractured, neon-scorched world depicted in the *Hollow Resonance* novella. Here, you will find the philosophical underpinnings of the setting, detailed descriptions of the city both before and after its cataclysmic transformation, a breakdown of the major factions and supernatural threats, and advice on how to utilize key personas and plot hooks to build your own Shadowrun campaigns.

Zkazena is a city defined by extremes. It is a place where ancient magic bleeds through the cracks of hyper-advanced corporate technology, where the line between life and death has been commodified, and where survival often demands terrible compromises. The narratives set here should reflect these harsh realities, exploring the resilience of those who call the ruins home and the monstrous entities that prey upon them.

### Core Philosophies

The world of *Hollow Resonance* is built upon several foundational themes that should inform every story, encounter, and character motivation within the setting.

#### Necessity Knows No Law
The maxim "Necessity knows no law" typically suggests moments of extreme duress where legal or ethical constraints are bypassed for survival. However, in the context of a world grappling with profound environmental devastation, unchecked corporate power, resurgent and alien supernatural forces, transformative technologies, and echoes of ancient existences, this phrase transcends proverbial status. It becomes a fundamental operating principle, a constant pressure shaping societies, driving conflicts, and defining the motivations of individuals, organizations, and even non-human entities. This world operates under the shadow of various necessities – the need to survive a poisoned environment, the corporate imperative for profit and control, the existential requirement to defend against inhuman horrors, the drive to adapt to or exploit radical biological and digital transformations, and the pull of ancient, non-metahuman imperatives. These forces routinely compel actions that exist outside, against, or simply orthogonal to the established legal and ethical frameworks designed for a simpler, more stable reality. The inherent conflict is not merely between legality and illegality, but between the fragile constructs of imposed order – laws, regulations, corporate policies, social norms – and the fundamental, often brutal, drivers of existence itself.

The pressures forcing entities beyond the bounds of law are diverse and interconnected. Environmental collapse, exemplified by ecological sacrifice zones like the Tar Creek Superfund site, creates desperate necessities for basic survival, forcing inhabitants into risky and illicit activities simply to endure. The rise of megacorporations, such as Wuxing Incorporated, establishes parallel systems of power where the 'laws' of profit and market dominance supersede public statutes, compelling employees and competitors alike into shadow economies and extralegal competition. Supernatural threats, ranging from the insidious infiltration of Insect Spirits to the potentially world-ending incursions of Horrors, demand responses so extreme – preemptive violence, forbidden magic, societal upheaval – that conventional legality becomes a secondary, often irrelevant, concern. Concurrently, technological advancements rewrite the very definition of life and necessity. Treatments like Leónization offer near-immortality to the elite, creating necessities tied to status and power maintenance that operate outside normal lifecycles. The HMHVV virus spawns new metahuman variants like ghouls, whose biological necessities clash with societal norms and laws. The emergence of Technomancers, Artificial Intelligences, and the exploitative technology of Bunraku parlors introduces new forms of consciousness, existence, and subjugation that defy easy legal categorization and control. Finally, the potential re-emergence of ancient beings like Obsidimen brings primordial necessities, tied to geological cycles and non-metahuman biology, into conflict with the structures of the modern world.

In such a complex and pressured environment, "Necessity Knows no Law" is not an exception but the pervasive rule. It dictates the flow of power, the struggle for resources, the nature of conflict, and the moral landscape inhabited by all beings. This analysis will explore how these various pressures, drawn from documented events and entities, weave together to create a world where the dictates of necessity consistently override the constraints of law, shaping a reality defined by extralegal imperatives and the constant struggle for existence.

#### Hopepunk
Hopepunk says that genuinely and sincerely caring about something, anything, requires bravery and strength. Hopepunk isn’t ever about submission or acceptance: It’s about standing up and fighting for what you believe in. It’s about standing up for other people. It’s about DEMANDING a better, kinder world, and truly believing that we can get there if we care about each other as hard as we possibly can, with every drop of power in our little hearts. Even in the darkest corners of Zkazena, characters should fight for sparks of humanity.

#### Live, Laugh, Love
A twist on Crowley's "Do What Thou Wilt." In this setting, consider the implications of:
- Do as god wilt
- Live as thou wilt
- Laugh as thou wilt
- Love as thou wilt

#### What Lies in the Balance?
Moral questions permeate the setting: Can any reparation for acts from the past make up for wrongs committed today? How long must a grudge endure? Until the last woman, man, and child have it wiped from their collective memories?

#### Horri or Horrors: What are they?
What makes Horrors so terrifying is that they appear reasonable, leading metahumanity to project human motives onto them. However, their motives are utterly alien and their logic foreign. We fool ourselves into believing our own reflection. They don't just destroy; they change everything, making something new and terrible.

#### Death is Just the Beginning
Growth incorporates pieces around it to remake them into something new. Something tired and old can have new life breathed back into it, but what if that growth is "un-natural"? Death is supposed to be final, but what if you could pick up the fallen pieces, fill in the gaps, and remake them?

#### Where's the Scapegoat?
The setting explores the cruelty of the unintended kind. The tragedy of being overwhelmed, underprepared, and taxed to the limit, yet demanded to give more. The price of failure is often paid by the innocent and hapless, while the powerful sidestep public wrath.

#### Soul of Punk
In a cyberpunk world, how do people push back? Losing isn't dying; it's selling out. Selling out is trading autonomy for rewards. The true punk spirit is maintaining that autonomy at any cost.

#### Death Becomes You
You can't truly die, but the alternatives are messy, offering few options for your extra life. You are not yourself anymore—a snipped thread tied into a new string. You look, smell, act, and feel wrong. The fear of that knot slipping undone drives you relentlessly. Being brought back means you owe someone big time, tied by life contracts, tokens, objects, or places. It's a terrifying, disorienting experience of being taken apart and put back together slightly wrong.

#### No Time Travel, No Teleportation, No Resurrection
Rules are meant to be bent, broken, and beaten.
- It’s not resurrection; you’re not you anymore.
- It’s not time travel; it’s causal effect happening out of order.
- It’s not teleportation; it’s always been here.
Teleportation involves creating fixed gates into an Astral metaplane, a costly and dangerous process. Time is always linear and forward; you may spend a lot of time in a metaplane and come out only minutes later, but never back in time.

#### Magic Exists Side-by-Side with Life
The manasphere ends at the atmosphere. Magic is intrinsically tied to the living world.

#### Who's Really Running the Show?
In a world managed by AIs vastly more intelligent than humans, what role do managers actually play in governance? Bringing AIs into the discussion is a social blunder, but when survival is on the line, new alliances must be formed. When a megacorp formally cuts ties to a subsidiary, blood is in the water.

#### Personafix
What if a BTL (Better-Than-Life) chip didn’t just work in the Matrix, but allowed you to take that experience into the real world? "Waking Dreams" let you walk around with a movie star or persona in your head, dictating your actions and emotions. These personafix chips transform personality, thoughts, and memories, creating severe identity crises and a booming, dangerous new market.

#### Leónization
A genetic anti-aging treatment that rejuvenates the body at the cellular level. This incredibly expensive combination of nanite gene therapy and cellular repair restores youth but comes with rumors of spontaneous cellular degeneration and sudden death after repeated treatments. It highlights the ultimate disparity between the wealthy elite and the struggling masses.

## Section 2: The Setting - Zkazena & The New Garden

Zkazena (also known colloquially as Crispyopolis) is a city that has undergone massive, fundamental shifts in a very short period. Game Masters can run campaigns in the "Before" state (a poisoned, corporate-controlled nightmare) or the "After" state (a mutated, magical ecosystem).

### The "Before" State: Crispyopolis
Previously known for its sulfur hot springs and deadly hydrogen sulfide fuming caves, Zkazena was originally considered sacred by Native Americans. In the 1900s, prospectors discovered cinnabar, birthing a mercury mining boomtown. The natural hot springs, thought to bring vigor, were later discovered to cause mercury poisoning. After the awakening, Wuxing purchased the land rights to resume mining and establish the Magellan Institute for Biomedical Technologies to manufacture reagents.

Under Wuxing, the town flourished until an outbreak of HMHVV nearly decimated it. Wuxing had utilized a specific strain that offered ghouls immunity to pathogens and toxins, allowing them to work effectively in the toxic mines. A subsequent "dirty bomb" incident was actually a cover story for a massive spike in background magic that created the dreaded "Containment Zone".

**Key Locations (Before):**
- **The White Tower:** A 1500-foot-tall arcology that gleams over the dark town. It houses people, businesses, and Wuxing operations. It acts as a massive spear of light in the pitch darkness.
- **Undercity:** Built directly beneath the White Tower by Wuxing geomancers, this natural cave system houses a heavily shielded Leónization facility. Ghouls work in the highly magically irradiated core to manufacture the raw ingredients.
- **The Old Mine:** Modeled after real-world ecological disasters like Tar Creek, this area features open pits and 7,000 acres of wasteland. Toxic spirits roam the area, and ghouls inhabit the ghost town and mine shafts.
- **The Containment Zone:** An environmental disaster zone with ultra-high background magic. It encompasses the Old Mines, the Citadel, and the White Tower. Mutations, echoes of form, and extreme reality distortions are common here.
- **The Citadel:** An area of distinct, ancient architecture with off-the-charts background magic. It is home to the Obsidimen, who are bound to the "Liferock" at the center of the primary ley line node.
- **Necropolis:** An ancient crypt near the Citadel housing high-magic, immortal dual-natured creatures (like Nosferatu) who were in stasis for millennia. They hold a non-aggression pact with the Obsidimen and work with the White Tower to provide secrets of eternal life in exchange for power.
- **Ole Miners Pharmacy:** One of the last holdout businesses in the ghost town portions of Zkazena, run by a pharmacist dedicated to helping the remaining stragglers.

### The "After" State: The New Garden
Following the events of *Hollow Resonance*, the Wuxing orbital strike ("Thor Shot") combined with the Architect's (Spark+) merging with the primary ley line node completely transformed Zkazena. The Architect's power diffused into the city's roots, acting as a passive "sleeping gardener" to maintain the atmosphere.

**Key Features (After):**
- **The Great Crystalline Tree:** The ruins of the White Tower ascended, pulled upward by the roots of a massive tree made of interwoven crystal and silver light.
- **The Crystalline Forest:** The brutalist corporate architecture has been softened and twisted, covered in a glowing, iridescent moss. Deep toxic fissures have transformed into glowing rivers of pure, liquid mana.
- **Warped Space-Time (Mana-Slips):** The New Garden's extreme magic warps space-time, creating "Mana-Slips." These allow individuals to travel rapidly through the crystalline moss, bypassing normal physical routes (e.g., slipping from the surface directly down into the Undercity).
- **The Risen Necropolis:** The ancient stone tombs of the Necropolis, previously buried deep underground, have been gently lifted to the surface and are now bathed in the light of the new forest.
- **The Immune Response:** The city itself now has an immune system. The localized reality manipulation can actively spawn massive rock/crystal constructs to defend the garden against aggressive intruders (like Wuxing Harvesters).

## Section 3: Factions & Threats

Zkazena is a battleground of conflicting ideologies, biological imperatives, and corporate greed.

### Wuxing Incorporated
A massive AAA-rated global megacorporation with a diversified portfolio spanning finance, magical goods, and shipping. In Zkazena, they act as the ultimate antagonist of necessity. Their mastery of geomancy (manipulating *qi*, *feng shui*, and mana flows) allows them to weaponize the environment itself.
- **Corporate Agenda:** Wuxing values profit and control above all. They treat the catastrophic Containment Zone not as a disaster to fix, but as a resource to exploit.
- **The Harvesters:** After the "New Garden" is formed, Wuxing pivots to extraction. They deploy specialized drones and "Null-Mages" (operatives trained to suppress ambient magic) to strip-mine the city's new crystalline mana formations.

### The Fuchsia Dragons
A highly dangerous, heavily armed mercenary marine company previously contracted by Ares, now operating independently (or attempting to secure resources) in the ruins of the White Tower. They are known as "The Roaches" due to their dark secret.
- **The Insect Pact:** The core of the company's power stems from a spirit pact with centipede insect spirits.
- **Procession of Remembrance:** When a heavy-hitter dies, the pact spirit devours them in a bloody explosion, transforming into a towering insect horror that retains the host's tactical memories. These entities are then encased in "Super Heavy" armor (black with neon pink, UV-reactive highlights) to hide their monstrous nature while they fight as the company's shock troops.
- **The Hive:** Over time, the company functions more like a hive mind directed by the monstrous spirits, though they maintain a human face (a newly chosen Captain) to handle negotiations.

### Ghouls and the "Waking Sleepers"
Ghouls make up a significant portion of Zkazena's population, forced to the margins by societal rejection and their biological imperatives (Strain III HMHVV).
- **Before the Garden:** They inhabit the Old Mines and the deep ruins, acting as scavengers or exploited laborers for Wuxing's Leónization core.
- **The Magical Surge:** When the Containment Zone expands (the "Sky Bleeds" event), the massive influx of raw magic drives the ghouls into a frenzied madness, turning them into a ravenous horde. However, as the mana stabilizes, strong-willed ghouls have the potential to revert to "perfect forms"—monstrous yet powerful reflections of their true selves, losing the constant, gnawing hunger.

### The Necropolis Vampires
Unlike traditional feral vampires, the inhabitants of the Necropolis are ancient, resplendent, and deeply entrenched in the city's power structure. They spent millennia in stasis waiting for ambient magic levels to rise. They possess intense political acumen and work directly with the White Tower's leadership, trading ancient secrets for influence.

### Obsidimen
Massive, rock-based humanoids composed of living stone. They are incredibly long-lived and fundamentally tied to a "Liferock" (the core of the primary ley line node). They operate on geological timescales, viewing the haste of metahumanity with confusion. If their Liferock or community is threatened, they become fearsome, unstoppable forces of nature.

### The Horrors (The Scourge)
The ultimate existential threat. Horrors are god-like, malevolent entities from deep metaplanes that cross over when ambient magic levels reach cyclical peaks. They feed on fear and suffering, and their presence warps reality. They cannot be reasoned with.
- In the events of *Hollow Resonance*, the uncapping of the ley line node nearly awakens an ancient Horri slumbering beneath Zkazena. The constant struggle of Zkazena's inhabitants is often an indirect (or direct) attempt to delay or prepare for their inevitable return.

### Nanites & AI
The Matrix is everywhere, and it is saturated with nanites.
- **The Gray Goo:** The atmosphere of Zkazena is thick with nanites, giving everyone a distinct Matrix signature and rendering traditional hacking obsolete.
- **Sprites & E-Ghosts:** Sprites are aggressive, personality-driven digital entities. E-ghosts (fragments of people who died online) haunt the digital ruins.
- **AIs:** Artificial Intelligences in this setting are often dual-natured, engaging in complex, world-altering machinations alongside dragons or corporate board members.

## Section 4: Key Personas & Campaign Hooks

Zkazena is populated by a cast of desperate, dangerous, and sometimes divine characters. GMs can use these personas as primary antagonists, uneasy allies, or narrative anchors.

### Primary Personas

*   **Kyber:** A jaded operative suffering from Cognitive Fragmentation Disorder (CFD), utilizing various personality constructs (e.g., 'Operator', 'Observer', 'Negotiator') to survive. *Use Kyber as a template for a complex protagonist or a deeply cynical NPC contact who knows the real cost of survival in Zkazena.*
*   **Logi (Prime & v2.1):** An "Archon of the Technorati," permanently infused with nanites. Logi Prime died to save the Architect, leaving behind a "Save State" (Logi v2.1) who lacks the memories of recent shared trauma (the 'Grief Gap'). *Use Logi to explore themes of identity, the loss of humanity to technology, and the terrifying coldness of pure, unburdened logic.*
*   **Cryptolock:** A ghoul rocker from the crispier side of town. Semi-famous for his apocalyptic heavy metal, he represents the "hopepunk" spirit of Zkazena. He uses Shadowrun slang heavily ("Fragging Nightmare"). *Use him as a local guide, a fiercely loyal (if cynical) ally, or a lens to explore the ghoul experience.*
*   **Francky:** A slick, predatory salesman peddling 'New Beginnings Life Insurance', which offers a horrific resurrection "return policy." He views people as seeds to be cultivated or discarded. *Use Francky as a charismatic, insidious early-game villain who uses grief and hope against the players.*
*   **Sister Sinalma:** A chillingly calm operative from the White Tower's Bio-Arcane Division. A bio-arcane genius and former cyber-surgeon who went rogue after intense cyberpsychosis. She seeks to initiate the "Chrysalis Protocol" to replace metahumanity with perfect "True Vessels." *Use her as a high-tier antagonist representing the darkest extremes of corporate R&D and magical fanaticism.*
*   **Spark+ (The Architect):** Initially a vulnerable human boy used as a battery, he merges with an alien "Wisp" and the ghost of Logi Prime to become a child-god. He ultimately merges with the primary ley line node to become the "sleeping gardener" of the New Garden. *Use Spark as the central MacGuffin or a terrifying, passive force of nature that the players must protect, appease, or stop.*
*   **Captain Lazlow & Nada'ira:** Leaders of the Fuchsia Dragons. Lazlow is a massive, spirit-possessed mercenary whose inevitable death transforms him into a towering insect horror. Nada'ira is the pragmatic, heavily armored lieutenant who takes over as the "human face" of the company after Lazlow's transformation. *Use them to force players into terrible moral compromises; they are effective allies but absolute monsters.*
*   **Johnny the Johnson:** A Personafix construct projected onto a variety of rental bodies. He is uploaded into the Matrix via data jack. *Use him as an uncanny, recurring quest giver whose physical appearance constantly changes but whose slick personality remains identical.*

### Campaign Story Arcs (The "Hollow Resonance" Path)

GMs can structure a campaign in Zkazena around the events of the *Hollow Resonance* novella.

**Act 1: Better Off Dead**
*   **The Hook:** An off-channel job involves the "resurrection" of dead community members (the Hollows).
*   **The Conflict:** Players investigate the emotional devastation of these impossible returns, leading them to Francky and his "New Beginnings" scam. The climax involves confronting Francky's "Resonator" machine, resulting in a massive magical backlash and the introduction of the mysterious "crystalise" (the Wisp).

**Act 2: Waking Dreams**
*   **The Hook:** Players must protect the newly formed crystalise and the vulnerable boy, Spark.
*   **The Conflict:** The players uncover Francky's "Project Nightingale" and Sister Sinalma's true agenda. The players must navigate the deep Undercity, discovering the ancient Citadel and Ley Lines. The act climaxes with Spark merging with the crystalise to become Spark+, the Architect.

**Act 3: The Sky Bleeds**
*   **The Hook:** A massive, reverse-temporal energy surge (the "meteorite") hits the city, vastly expanding the Containment Zone.
*   **The Conflict:** The expansion triggers a mass frenzy among the ghouls, who lay siege to the White Tower. The players must survive the chaos as the Fuchsia Dragons arrive, ostensibly as saviors but actually as an occupying force with a predatory agenda.

**Act 4: The Siege of White Tower**
*   **The Hook:** The players are trapped inside the besieged White Tower during a total Matrix blackout.
*   **The Conflict:** Wuxing decides to cut its losses and prepares an orbital bombardment ("Thor Shot") to glass the city. The players must negotiate with trapped AI, fight off internal threats (like a desperate Francky or Sinalma), and decide whether to escape or "man the walls" to defend Spark+.

**Act 5: Apotheosis**
*   **The Hook:** The orbital strike is initiated, but Spark+ absorbs it, triggering a cataclysmic magical event.
*   **The Climax:** The energy shatters the seals on the ancient ley line nodes, awakening the Necropolis and potentially ancient Horrors. The players must make a final, defining choice regarding Spark+: allow him to merge with the city to create the "New Garden", attempt to control him, or try to escape the newly transformed, highly magical, and incredibly dangerous Zkazena.
